**The key lesson (changes how we title and frame everything):** viewers click on **the experience** ("you wouldn't last", "what it was like", "every rank"), not on "prices". **Money is our hook inside the video and our differentiator, not the headline topic.** Every title leads with the human experience; the price is the twist.

**Why it pays:** Education/history long-form earns some of YouTube's highest RPMs. The Partner Program's long-form route (1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months) is far more reachable for a new faceless channel than the Shorts route (10M Shorts views in 90 days). Long-form is the money; Shorts are discovery.

**Why it suits 7AI:** narration + illustrated stills is the whole format. Every episode follows one structure but needs genuinely new research. That keeps us clear of YouTube's "inauthentic / mass-produced content" policy.

## 5. The money method (non-negotiable)

1. **Primary unit: days of an ordinary labourer's wage** in that place and time. Every price becomes "about X days of work".
2. **Today's money:** multiply by **$120 per day** (8 hours at about $15/hour, a typical US entry-level wage). Say this once per long video, within the first 60 seconds, and show it on the price card.
3. **Ranges, not false precision.** "About 3 days' pay", never "2.87 days".
4. **Never mix eras.** Prices and wages in one video come from the same period. *(Example: Diocletian's Price Edict is from 301 AD, after a century of inflation. It can't be combined with a 1st-century soldier's pay.)*
5. **Official vs real prices:** if a source is an official maximum or an estimate, say so ("the official maximum price", "estimates range from…").
6. **When sources disagree, give the range and say so.**

**Preferred source types:**

| Era / region | Sources |
|---|---|
| Roman Empire | Army pay records and studies (225 denarii/yr under Augustus, 300 under Domitian); Pompeii price graffiti (79 AD); Diocletian's Edict (301 AD only) |
| Medieval & early-modern Europe | Academic wage and price series (Robert Allen, Gregory Clark), Phelps Brown–Hopkins index |
| Mughal India | *Ain-i-Akbari*; Irfan Habib, Shireen Moosvi |
| Victorian Britain | Mayhew's *London Labour and the London Poor* (1851); Rowntree's York survey (1899); period newspapers |
| Edo Japan | Rice-based (koku) wage and price studies |

## 6. Script rules

### 6.1 All videos
- Concrete always: a named place, a year, an object, a number.
- Short sentences: ≤ 20 words for Shorts, ≤ 25 for long-form.
- **Banned openers:** "Did you know", "In this video", "Hey guys", "Throughout history", "Let's talk about", "Welcome back".
- **Banned endings:** "Like and subscribe", "Follow for more", "Let me know in the comments", a question, any moral about modern life.
- **First sentence** states place + year (or era) + a striking number or claim.

### 6.2 Long-form, Format A beat sheet (8–10 min)

| Time | Chapter | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Cold open | "You wake up in [place], [year]. You're a [role]. You earn [wage]. By the end of this video you'll know if that makes you comfortable or desperate." |
| 0:30–1:00 | Method + map | The money method in one sentence; a map card |
| 1–3 min | Your work and wage | What you do all day; how and how much you're paid |
| 3–4:30 | Your home | Where you live, what it costs |
| 4:30–6 | What you eat | One day's food, priced |
| 6–7:30 | The thing you dream of | One luxury and how many days or years it costs |
| 7:30–9 | The crisis | A real risk of the era (tax, famine, debt, illness) and its cost |
| last 30s | Final tally | "In today's money, your year's pay is about $X: roughly what [modern comparison] earns in [period]." Pause 1 second before it. Then stop. |

- **Re-hooks:** every chapter opens with a question it answers ("So can you afford meat?"), and roughly every 2 minutes ends with a forward pull ("But your real problem arrives in October.").
- **Formats B and C** follow the same chapter discipline. B: strangest detail → the job → a day doing it → pay → why it existed → what killed it → where the workers went. C: one chapter per rank, bottom to top, each with role, pay in days, one lifestyle detail, and a final "who actually had it best" twist.

### 6.3 Shorts
Follow the S1/S2/S3 structures in §4.2. The strongest fact goes last. ~150 words per 60s, ~110 per 45s.

## 7. Accuracy & sensitivity

- **Every price, wage and date needs a source.** At least 2 sources per video, at least one academic or primary.
- **Composite characters are fine; invented facts are not.** "You" is a typical person; every detail must be documented as typical. The long-form description says so.
- **Myths are framed as myths** ("legend says", "no ancient source says").
- **Slavery, caste, famine, violence:** covered factually and soberly. Never a fun role to "play", never a punchline.
- No gore beyond one clause. No current politics, no nationalist framing, religion described neutrally.
- **Topic research test:** a long-form topic needs ≥ 5 sourced prices or wages from the same period. Fewer → it's a Short.

## 11. How to write a Short brief (current pipeline)

The pipeline turns your brief into a short **topic** plus a list of **constraints**, then discards the original text (until Sprint 4 Task 2). So:

- Write **one `Constraint:` line per instruction**, with the facts *inside* the lines.
- Make the **Topic** concrete and visual (role + place + era). The image planner only sees the topic and the script.
- **Keep sources out of the brief.** Keep them in §13 and paste them into the YouTube description after upload.

**Template**
```
Topic: <ordinary role or everyday thing>, <place>, <year or era>.
Constraint: about <110 for 45s | 150 for 60s> words, short sentences.
Constraint: first sentence must be exactly: "<hook with place/year and a number>"
Constraint: facts in order: (1) … (2) … (3) … (4, strongest) …
Constraint: count <same-era labourer's daily wage> as one day's work; by our rule a day's work is $120 today. Say dollar figures as the value of days of work, never as the value of the coins. Use "about".
Constraint: use these exact hedge words: "<mostly / largely / estimated / official maximum / no ancient source>".
Constraint: end on fact 4. No moral, no question, no call to action.
```

**Story brief template** (used from Q2/Q4 on):
```
Topic: <person, object or moment>, <place>, <year or era>: <3-4 visual scene ideas>.
Constraint: about <110 for 45s | 150 for 60s> words. Mix short punchy sentences with a few longer ones.
Constraint: tell it as a story in the beats below, joined with "but" and "so". Do not list facts.
Constraint: use only the facts in these beats; add no other facts, numbers or details.
Constraint: first sentence must be exactly: "<hook>"
Constraint: beat 1: <setup>
Constraint: beat 2: so <consequence>
Constraint: beat 3 (the turn): but <complication>
Constraint: beat 4: so <resolution>
Constraint: use these exact hedge words: "<...>".
Constraint: last sentence must be exactly: "<kicker that calls back to the hook>"
```

**Lessons from real runs** (details: `7AI/docs/review_tuning/SCRIPT_REVIEW_MISSES.md`):
- **Conversions are a rule, not a value.** Never write "X coins = $Y" in a brief; the script will repeat it as a false fact (Case 2).
- **Write the exact hedge words.** The writer copies hedges literally and drops any you leave implicit ("mostly", "largely", "estimated").
- **Keep TTS-risky words out of the brief:** "gaol" → "jail"; the Roman coin "as/asses" → "small bronze coins"; spell out awkward Latin only when it's the point of the Short.
- **Name the key term** when a fact depends on it ("say the word 'salary'").
- **No same-era labourer wage in the sources → no dollar figure.** Leave the conversion out rather than borrow a wage from another era.
- **Story briefs must say "use only the facts in these beats; add no other facts, numbers or details."** Without it the writer invents vivid colour (Cases 4 and 5: "Whole villages lost half their people", "Blood was shed over bread and wine").
- **Every brief ships with a ready Description block** (summary line, how we converted, sources with links, credit line, hashtags). The pipeline writes the YouTube description from the script only, so this is pasted in Studio after upload.

## 12. Checklists

**Gate 1 (script):**
- [ ] First sentence has place + year + a number or claim
- [ ] Every number matches the brief exactly
- [ ] Hedges present, word for word as in the brief (mostly / largely / estimated / maximum price / myth); no "none", "all", "never" the brief didn't say
- [ ] Dollar figures describe **days of work**, never the coins themselves ("a day's work, about $120", not "a penny was worth $120")
- [ ] No TTS-risky words (gaol, asses, unexplained Latin)
- [ ] No banned opener or ending
- [ ] Under the word count

If any box fails, **edit the script text and approve** rather than rerunning. **Don't rely on the review score**: it gave 9/10 to both Q1 and Q4 despite factual errors. Paste the edit **before** tapping Approve; the narration is recorded from the approved text.

**Gate 2 (video):**
- [ ] No photoreal faces, no modern objects, no text inside images
- [ ] Images match the era stated
- [ ] Captions readable; audio level fine
- [ ] Nothing gory or mocking

**After upload (YouTube Studio):**
- Paste the brief's **Description block** (§13) into the description, replacing the auto-generated one
- Add to the "Shorts: Price Facts" playlist
- Altered content = yes
