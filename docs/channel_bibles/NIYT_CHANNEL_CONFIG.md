# Channel Production Spec (working name: Not In Your Textbook)

**Version 2.1 · 2026-10-18 · Owner: Neeraj · written by the architect chat.**
**Read this first.** Any chat that writes a brief, sets a channel option or judges a video for this channel starts here. It says what the channel is, the full production settings a ten-person team would hold, which of them the app supports today, the exact values to enter, how a brief (a "treatment") is built, and the gap list that feeds Sprint 6 and 7.

**What changed from v1.0 (same day):** the channel is no longer myth checks and fact lists. It is **immersive, first-person-style history**. "The Price of the Past" is parked (it stays in the old playbook, `NOT_IN_YOUR_TEXTBOOK.md`, unused). v1.0 also had two errors, now fixed: the description template supports only `{summary}`, `{sources}`, `{editor}` and `{hashtags}`, and `editorCredit` in the profile is on/off, not a name.

**What changed from v2.0 (same day):** the channel now tells **stories people can feel**, with a documentary's commentary between scenes (§2a). The style guide, the look and the title guidance were rewritten (§7). The painted look is replaced by a **documentary frame** look that is on trial until the first Gate 2 look test (§10, §14). Titles are 35 to 55 characters and carry one soft link to today that the script itself contains (§11). Treatments gain an arc, a feeling per chapter and an echo (§9). New: draft series format under G2 and gap G16, look per series (§13).

**How to read the tags** (every setting carries one; update them when the app ships a feature):
- ✅ **Wired**: a field in the app (profile or New brief). Enter it.
- 📝 **Text**: the app has no field. It is carried in the style guide or brief lines and a person checks it at the gates.
- ⬜ **Future**: needs an app feature, listed in §13. Never treat it as active.

## 1. The concept

**Working name:** Not In Your Textbook. The name is not final (§14). Shortlist: **Through Their Eyes**, **Elsewhen** (elsewhere + when), or keep the name with a new tagline. Renaming in the app, on YouTube and in channel settings is free at any time; tests do not depend on it. Decide before the first Public upload.

**Domain:** lived experience across human history and cultures. One point of view, at one place and one moment, built from research and carried by imagination, told with respect. Early humans to the recent past, plus living communities under strict rules.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Promise | *History from the inside.* |
| Audience | English-speaking, global, 18–45, curious generalists who like a documentary feel |
| Tone | A story first, a documentary's commentary second: calm, vivid, felt, respectful. Gentle humour at most. Says "we do not know" without embarrassment. |
| Language | English |
| Products | **Long** 8–10 min, 16:9 (the core) · **Short** 30–60 s, 9:16 (discovery and topic tests) |
| Disclosure | Every video says it is imagined (§2) |
| Not this channel | Famous-ruler biographies, textbook summaries, ancient-aliens content, anything mocking a people, current politics, nationalist or racial framing, gore for effect, hypothetical future attacks |

## 2. Three layers of truth (every video, every fact)

| Layer | What it is | Who may state it |
|---|---|---|
| **Documented** | Sourced facts with their hedge words ("about", "may", "according to one count") | Any stance; hedges kept |
| **Inferred** | Typical for that time and place, but archaeologists hedge it (for example "presumably bread") | A first-person narrator may state it plainly *only because the day is declared imagined*. The closing chapter restates it as "probably". |
| **Imagined** | What the writer may invent: the narrator's thoughts, feelings, wants and small worries, body sensations, weather, light, sounds, smells, small gestures, unnamed neighbours, and up to two short everyday speech lines by unnamed people (§2a) | Only inside this list. **No invented numbers, dates, names, places, prices, rules, beliefs or rituals.** |

Plus a fourth bucket: **Commentary-only** facts (debated, hedged, or knowledge from after the scene, like "the script is still undeciphered"). A first-person narrator cannot know these. Only the commentary voice states them, hedges kept.

**Disclosure, in three places:** (1) the exact spoken first sentence says it is imagined and built from real finds; (2) the description template carries the creative-liberty line on every video (✅ automatic); (3) the pinned comment repeats it (manual). Longs end with 30–45 seconds on what was documented, inferred and imagined.

## 2a. Story feel and voices

People watch for a person they can feel and one true thing they can repeat. Feeling is imagined, so it lives in the Imagined layer; facts keep their layers.

**Story craft** (carried by the guide in §7 and by each treatment): one person at one place and moment who wants something small and has something at stake · an arc for the video and one dominant feeling and one turn per chapter (the treatment names them) · feeling shown through the body and senses, never named ("what the hands do", not "I felt nervous") · every fact tied to an action, an object in hand or a consequence, never a list · one concrete, surprising detail per chapter · each chapter opens with a question and answers it · written for the ear: one idea per sentence, a short sentence at emotional peaks, no semicolons · the ending echoes the opening, with no moral, question or call to action.

**Commentary** is the calm documentary voice. It explains what the scene just showed and names the real find or source behind it, hedges kept.

**People talking.** Documentaries cut to people speaking on screen. We have one voice, so it is done in the script:

| Kind | Allowed | How today | Later |
|---|---|---|---|
| A real quote | A short quote, word for word, in the treatment with its source named | The narrator reads it with attribution; in Shorts the burned-in captions show it | Second voice and a name-and-title slate (G6, G7) |
| Everyday speech | Up to two short lines by unnamed people in a scene, each with a speech tag ("my neighbour says"); no facts, numbers or names | Same voice | Character voice (G6) |
| A scholar's view | An attributed paraphrase taken from the treatment | Commentary voice | Quote card (G7) |
| A real, named or historical person | Never invent their words | | |

**Then and now.** Pictures blend the two: scene chapters show the world of the story (hands, objects, light); commentary chapters show the place as it is today (ruins at dawn, a landscape). The treatment's picture line sets it.

## 3. The stance system

Narration and pictures each have a stance. They can match or deliberately counterpoint. Any two or all three can be mixed.

| Stance | Narration | Pictures |
|---|---|---|
| **First person** | "I" inside the scene. Present tense. | The camera is the narrator's eyes: hands, tools, thresholds, the horizon. The narrator's face is never shown. |
| **Second person** | "You" and "come with me". A guide speaks to a visitor. Present tense. | The camera is the viewer: a guide gestures toward it, objects are held out, over-the-shoulder views. |
| **Third person / commentary** | An outside voice that says what is known and not known. Past tense. | Observer: wide establishing views, people as silhouettes or from behind, reconstructions. Maps and diagrams (⬜). |

**Rules**
1. Each treatment lists a **stance plan per chapter** (narration and pictures).
2. Switch stance only at chapter boundaries. Shorts may switch at the first and last sentence only. At most 3 switches in 10 minutes.
3. A switch is marked by a sound cue (⬜) until then by a hard cut to a different picture type.
4. The narrator is a **composite** and **nameless** unless the sources name a person. Age, role and place are stated in the treatment. No accents or dialect caricature. No invented quotes from real people.
5. Present tense inside the scene, past tense in commentary. Sentences at most 25 words.
6. **No anachronism inside the scene.** A first-person or second-person narrator never uses modern units, dates or terms (grams, metres, BCE, "years ago", archaeologists, civilization). Only the commentary voice does. A fact stated in modern units is given by the commentary voice, or the narrator rephrases it in their own terms.
7. **Commentary is the documentary voice**: calm; it explains what the scene just showed and names the real find or source; it never invents feeling for a real person.

## 5. Sensitivity levels (set per video in the treatment)

| Level | Examples | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Low** | Long-dead cultures: Indus cities, Vikings, early humans | Imagination allowed within the layers |
| **2 Medium** | Ancient wars and peoples with living descendants | Neutral; no side taken; no claim that a modern group is the heir or enemy of an ancient one |
| **3 High** | Recent events with survivors: Hiroshima and Nagasaki 1945, Trinity test 1945, other modern disasters | Built on documented survivor and eyewitness accounts; no gore; no recreated deaths of identifiable people; the human experience, not spectacle; extra time at Gate 1 and 2 |
| **4 Highest** | Living peoples, including those living apart from the modern world | No invented inner life, beliefs or rituals for a real people; use published ethnography; never give locations; never show sacred rites or a recognisable individual; present tense; names the people use for themselves; never an early video |

Always: human remains one clause, no lingering; religion neutral; no hypothetical modern attacks; the Aryan-invasion and migration debate only through named studies, hedged, never a headline hook. At levels 3 and 4 the feeling comes from silence and ordinary detail, never from invented inner life for a real named person.

**Style guide (`guide`; the writer and the reviewer read it):** story feel, truth layers, voices, stance and respect in one text (3,070 characters; the profile schema has no length limit; if the app refuses it, move the Voices and Respect paragraphs into the treatment).
> Channel: immersive, research-based history from the inside. Tell it as a story a person can feel, built only on facts that are true. A story follows one person at one place and moment who wants something small and has something at stake (pride in their work, worry about the weather, care for someone). Give the video an emotional arc and each chapter one dominant feeling and one turn; the brief may name them; deliver them. Show feeling through the body and the senses (hands, breath, heat, smell, sound), never by naming it: not "I felt nervous", but what the hands do. Make every fact matter by tying it to an action, an object in hand or a consequence; never recite facts as a list. One concrete, surprising detail per chapter. Each chapter opens with a question and answers it. Write for the ear: one idea per sentence, at most 25 words, a short sentence at emotional peaks, full stops and commas as pauses, no semicolons or brackets. No superlatives or hype words unless the brief has them. End on an image or line that echoes the opening, never a moral, question or call to action. If the brief gives an exact first or last sentence, use it word for word.
>
> Truth: use only the facts in the brief for anything presented as real. The brief has lists: DOCUMENTED (sourced facts; keep every hedge word), INFERRED (typical for the time and place; a first-person narrator may state them plainly, never as certainty in commentary), COMMENTARY-ONLY (debated or later knowledge; only the commentary voice may state it, hedges kept) and IMAGINED (the only things you may invent). Feelings, wants, worries, small personal stakes, sensations and everyday speech are imagined and allowed within the IMAGINED list. Never invent numbers, dates, names, places, prices, rules, beliefs or rituals. Never make a fact bigger or smaller for effect, and never present an imagined detail as documented.
>
> Voices: people may speak. Allowed: short quotes given word for word in the brief with their source named, and up to two short lines of everyday speech by unnamed people in a scene, each with a speech tag ("my neighbour says"), containing no facts, numbers or names. Never invent words for a real, named or historical person or scholar; a scholar's view is an attributed paraphrase from the brief.
>
> Stance: follow the brief's stance plan per chapter (first person "I", second person "you", third-person commentary); switch only at chapter boundaries; present tense in the scene, past tense in commentary. Inside the scene never use modern units, dates or terms (grams, metres, BCE, years ago, archaeologists). Never name a nameless narrator. Commentary is the calm documentary voice: it explains what the scene just showed and names the real find or source behind it. Say plainly when something is unknown or debated; never settle a debate the brief leaves open.
>
> Respect: calm, vivid, never mocking, no stereotypes, no gore. For recent or sensitive events let silence and ordinary detail carry the feeling; no suffering as spectacle; no invented inner life for a real named person.

## 9. The treatment (what a brief contains)

A treatment is the per-video creative plan. It is sent to the pipeline as `Topic:` and `Constraint:` lines (the app reads text only). The sound plan is kept in the doc and **not** pasted: sound cues in the brief would be read aloud.

Fields: concept · persona · sensitivity level · **arc** (the feeling journey in one line) · stance plan per chapter (narration and pictures) · **feeling and turn per chapter** (Longs) · exact first sentence · exact last sentence · chapter titles · per chapter the DOCUMENTED, INFERRED, COMMENTARY-ONLY lists · IMAGINED allowance (§2) · **echo** (the one present-day link the script must carry in a commentary sentence; the exact last sentence can be the echo) · picture direction, then and now (in the `Topic:` line, which the picture planner reads) · sources (URLs for the Sources field) · sound plan (doc only, ⬜). The arc, feelings and echo are pasted as `Constraint:` lines.

**The title writer sees only the script and the title guidance, not the brief.** A link to today works in a title only if the script contains it, so the echo is planted in the script, not in a separate brief field.

**How a treatment is made:** (1) topic pick; (2) the architect chat researches and builds a **fact table** (exact wording, hedges, source tag, "verify" flag); (3) **you strike anything you do not trust**, this is the real fact-check; (4) the treatment is written from the surviving rows; (5) run; (6) Gate 1 against the table; (7) Gate 2; (8) Private upload, check, Public by hand. About 3 documented facts per minute of video. If the table is smaller, make the video shorter.

## 10. Checklists

**Gate 1 (script):**
- [ ] First and last sentences exact
- [ ] Every number and name matches the fact table; nothing outside the lists
- [ ] Imagined content stays inside the IMAGINED allowance (no invented numbers, names, places, beliefs, rituals)
- [ ] Hedged facts: in commentary chapters the hedge word is kept; inferred items used in first-person chapters are restated as "probably" in the closing chapter
- [ ] The stance plan was followed (narration switches only at chapter boundaries)
- [ ] Scene chapters contain no modern units, dates or terms (grams, metres, BCE, "years ago", archaeologists); commentary-only facts appear only in commentary chapters
- [ ] Chapters: 3 or more, each opens with its question; closing chapter says what was documented, inferred and imagined
- [ ] Story feel: each chapter has its one feeling and one turn; the feeling shows in hands, senses or small actions, never in a label ("nervous", "relieved")
- [ ] Every fact is tied to an action, an object or a consequence; no list of facts
- [ ] Any speech is a verbatim treatment quote with its source named, or at most two everyday lines with a speech tag and no facts, numbers or names
- [ ] Commentary explains what the scene just showed and names the find; the echo sentence is present
- [ ] No banned opener or ending, no moral, no question, no call to action
- [ ] Word count in range; hard names the voice may stumble on (Mohenjo-daro, Lagash) noted
- [ ] Edit in the sheet before Approve (narration is recorded from the approved text)

**Gate 2 (video):**
- [ ] First-person chapters show hands, tools and views at eye level; no recurring face
- [ ] No writing, symbols, logos or lettering in any picture
- [ ] Nothing violent, no human remains, nothing mocking, nothing sacred of a living people
- [ ] Period and place fit what is being narrated at that moment
- [ ] Commentary chapters show the place today (ruins, a landscape); scene chapters show the story's world
- [ ] **Look test (first Short only):** did one frame make you feel something? does any frame look like generic AI? do hands and faces hold up at phone size? If the second answer is yes or the third is no, look A fails: record it in §14 and re-run S-1 once with look B
- [ ] The spoken disclaimer is clear in the first 10 seconds
- [ ] Voice says hard names acceptably; music does not fight the voice
- [ ] Length within the floor (8:00 for a 10-minute video); ending clean; `.srt` downloads
- [ ] Watched in full with sound

**Note what the current flow cannot do** (stance of the pictures, ambience, emotion in the music, voice direction). Send it as `G: <what>`; it goes into §13.

## 11. Packaging

- **Title:** one human moment plus one soft link to today, taken from the script (§7 title guidance). 35 to 55 characters, Shorts under 45; optional bracket tag "(Imagined)" when it fits. Why: curiosity wording is the most consistent title winner in 2026 title data, 30 to 50 characters is the best-supported length, and "day in the life" and "modern echo" are listed working history shapes. Honest, specific titles also set the channel apart from the flood of AI "sleep history". Suggestions are in Appendix A.
- **Thumbnail (manual, 1280x720):** one frame from the video that shows one object in hand or a then-and-now contrast (a hand and a small stone cube, a street at dawn), at most 4 words, same typeface and corner tag every time. Make two variants and use YouTube's Test and Compare on Longs (it excludes Shorts). The custom-thumbnail 403 is open.
