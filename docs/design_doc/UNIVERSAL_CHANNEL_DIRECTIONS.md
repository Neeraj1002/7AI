# Puzzle & Reveal and Industrial Explainer for 7AI

**Product and architecture proposal · 27 November 2026.** Add two opt-in creative directions. Choose **Puzzle & Reveal** when the viewer gets to make a judgment and revisit it in light of evidence. Choose **Industrial Explainer** when the viewer should leave able to explain a system's behavior or predict what changes under a different condition. Keep the four existing directions. Use the existing channel note and episode brief to vary the experience within each family.

## Decision on every existing preset

These recommendations assess the actual v1 instructions and their consumers on the integration branch. The main checkout alone cannot establish an output-quality difference among the four settings.

| Current key | Overlap with Puzzle & Reveal | Overlap with Industrial Explainer | Decision and reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| History documentary · `history` | Plants questions, opens in a documented moment, and settles what the sources permit. | Can cover infrastructure or the origins of a process. | **KEEP.** Conflict, period and place organize the narrative. Its picture review explicitly checks for anachronism. A historical account can succeed even when there is no solvable question or mechanism to teach. |
| Story first · `story-first` | Strongest narrative overlap. It opens at a strange moment, follows a central question and pays it off. | Can follow a designer or team through a consequential decision. | **KEEP.** A person's goal, obstacle, consequent action and turn can carry a true story. The viewer need not receive advance evidence or propose a solution. Folding it into Puzzle would weaken both promises. |
| Explainer · `explainer` | An odd step can become a discovery. The existing ending can echo the hook. | Strongest subject overlap. Its instructions already cover how something works or is made. | **KEEP.** Its unit is an observable action and concrete detail in sequence. A raw-material-to-shelf video can finish the job without comparing designs or modeling system behavior. The repository's Start to Shelf brief documents this exact use. Industrial should earn its place on causal depth, including cases where sequence alone misleads. [Channel specification](../channel_bibles/START_TO_SHELF_CHANNEL_CONFIG.md) |
| Neutral · `neutral` | Allows a question-led brief. | Allows a process-led brief. | **KEEP.** Its empty domain, story shape and chapter style leave those choices to the creator. It is also the resolver default for earlier and absent directions. Removing it would change the meaning of existing data. |

No existing direction warrants a merge, deprecation or removal in this release. The repository records intended assignments of History to Not In Your Textbook, Story first to Cold Open, and Explainer to Start to Shelf. Those assignments are deployment plans, not a current tenant inventory. Retiring a key in a later release would require inspecting active **and historical** profile references, stored run options, retries and old clients, preserving read/replay compatibility, and offering an explicit new profile version to owners. Usage counts alone would miss the different promise that Start to Shelf documents. [Assignment report](../execution/sprint6/reports/task4.md)

## Creative specifications

| Decision | Puzzle & Reveal | Industrial Explainer |
|---|---|---|
| Viewer promise | “Here is enough to start thinking. See what you can work out, then check your reasoning.” Works for observation, hidden incentives, historical investigation, surprising connections and open design problems. | “Here is the behavior. Follow the parts and conditions that produce it.” Works for manufacturing, materials, infrastructure, logistics, software processes, service systems and technological evolution. |
| Opening | Put the object, situation or contradiction in view. Ask a specific, answerable question or offer a choice. Establish what the viewer is entitled to infer at this point. | Demonstrate a visible effect, design feature or operating constraint early. Name the system and bound the explanation tightly enough to follow it. Revealing the output immediately often helps. |
| Middle and retention | Give the viewer something to do before answering. Each new observation changes a plausible prediction, eliminates an alternative or exposes a missing relationship. Offer a partial answer when earned. An invitation can be a silent look or a comparison in narration. | Trace one consequential path through the system. Establish what enters, what changes, what causes that change, and what the next part receives. Pause at the decision or feedback relationship that explains the outcome. Use a supported change of conditions to make a difficult link intelligible. |
| Ending | Return to the original evidence. Demonstrate the inference, trace the hidden transfer, or compare the proposed arrangement with the stated constraints. Say which questions remain open. | Revisit the opening effect using the causal model. Show what the design achieves, what limits it, or how the output would differ under a supported alternative. A counterfactual is useful only when the evidence licenses it. |
| Short and long cadence | A Short can use one observation, room to inspect it and a callback. In long form, settle intermediate questions as they become answerable and carry the remaining uncertainty into the next chapter. No compulsory countdown or last-second reveal. | A Short can explain a single link in depth. In long form, give each chapter a subsystem, transformation or design choice and connect its output to the next. Cycles can return to an earlier state. A list of steps does not explain feedback. |
| Script review | Can a reader identify the question, their opportunity to reason, the evidence available at that moment, and the line that resolves it? For a unique answer, can a second plausible answer be excluded? For an open challenge, does the solution meet the constraints at a stated cost? | Can a reader explain how one change causes another? Is an important handoff missing? Does the proposed design rationale have support, or should it be labeled a possible benefit? Can a reader use the explanation to account for the opening observation? |
| Visual review | Use stable reference points. Keep a clue readable at playback size without relying on tiny text or color alone. Check for early spoilers and preserve geometry between setup and reveal. Cash flow can use stable participants and countable tokens. A room needs the same orientation and fixed boundaries in both views. | Establish a whole-system view when needed, then isolate the component performing the narrated action. Keep direction, scale and before/after states intelligible. Use separate still states where motion is unavailable. Identify a schematic simplification if it changes what a viewer could infer. |
| Failure conditions | Withheld premises, an unobservable clue, a twist supplied by the narrator alone, false certainty about motives, or a workable design called the only answer. | A montage of adjacent steps without causes, unexplained jargon, invented dimensions or efficiencies, an illustration presented as documentary proof, or an inferred advantage presented as the designer's stated intent. |

**Research and factual integrity.** The brief should carry the source passage, measurement with units and conditions, or worked assumptions needed for the decisive claim. Preserve uncertainty from the source. A fictional puzzle can stipulate its rules, a hypothetical purchase can stipulate prices, and a room can stipulate clearances. Identify those premises as constructed. For an actual transaction, distinguish amounts paid, redistributed and retained from profit. For an origin story, distinguish contemporaneous evidence of intent from a modern explanation of usefulness. Missing evidence should narrow the conclusion. A plausible model review is insufficient to establish a fact.

## How the seven concepts fit

| Channel or direction | Recommended starting point | How one episode earns its ending |
|---|---|---|
| A · The Chokepoint | **Puzzle & Reveal** for a hidden dependency. **Industrial** for a known dependency whose operation matters. | In a fictional bookbinding queue, several orders wait although their covers are ready. Let viewers deduce the shared drying rack from the work tickets. An Industrial treatment names the rack early and shows how its occupancy shapes throughput. |
| B · Built That Way | **Industrial Explainer** | Show the unusual feature on an everyday object. Show its behavior under the relevant constraint and the trade-off of another design. Puzzle also fits when viewers can infer the feature's purpose from evidence they see first. |
| C · Invisible Rules | **Puzzle & Reveal** | Offer a choice that looks irrational from one participant's perspective, then disclose relevant incentives in sequence. Return to the choice with a qualified explanation. Industrial fits an episode tracing a known allocation rule across a whole service. |
| D · Origin Engine | **Industrial Explainer** for design evolution. | Connect a limitation of an earlier version to a documented change and its effect. Use Puzzle for a surprising provenance supported by clues, History for a period account, or Story first when a person's struggle carries the episode. |
| E · Fair-Play Puzzles / One Clue Club | **Puzzle & Reveal**, observational | One mystery, one decisive pictured clue. Establish the allowed choices, show the complete illustration, allow inspection, then return to the same detail and explain why it settles the choice. A cold viewer must be able to point to it before the reveal. |
| F · Follow the Transaction / Money Explainers | **Puzzle & Reveal**, discovery | Start at a familiar purchase and ask who funds the apparent benefit. Switch from buyer to business, trace the payment and distinguish a discount from a reimbursement. Reconcile the amounts on returning to the receipt. The viewer need not know a hidden contract before it is disclosed. |
| G · Design Challenge / Room for Both | **Puzzle & Reveal**, open challenge | Show one room, the fixed features and both needs. Let viewers form a layout. Demonstrate one arrangement, revisit each need and show the compromise. Multiple valid answers strengthen this comparison. Industrial fits an episode explaining how the folding mechanism itself works. |

Choose the primary direction by the **last question the viewer can answer**. A hybrid may use a short causal explanation inside a reveal, or invite a prediction inside a systems lesson. Only one direction supplies the governing arc. A note such as “Let viewers try a layout before the walkthrough” can persist across a channel. Room dimensions, the key clue and payment terms belong to individual briefs. There is no mode picker, classifier, blended preset or requirement to use a detective voice.

### Same evidence, different viewing experience

Consider a fictional sorter with two trays. Its discs have the same diameter but different thicknesses. The thin ones fall through a slot. The thick ones continue across it to the end tray. These are hand-written editorial examples using only those premises.

**Puzzle & Reveal.** “One disc reaches the end tray. The other drops out of sight. From above, they're the same size. Watch them edge on as they reach the gap. Which one will make it across? The thicker disc does. Look back at that gap. The thin edge fits through it. The thick edge can't.” The edit must actually supply the edge view before the question is settled. Remove that shot and the fair-play promise fails even though every sentence is true.

**Industrial Explainer.** “This sorter separates discs by thickness. Follow one along the track. A thin disc fits through the slot and falls into the tray below. A thicker edge spans the opening, so that disc travels on to the end tray. Two discs can look identical from above and still arrive in different trays. It is their clearance at the slot that decides the route.” The result can be named in the first sentence. Remove the clearance explanation and the pictures show two destinations without explaining what selects between them.

This comparison supports keeping two directions at the design level. Its conclusion is testable with a generated pair. A blind viewer of the first should be able to say what they used to predict the answer. A viewer of the second should be able to predict an unfamiliar disc's route given its thickness relative to the opening. Audience retention and automated render reliability require separate observations.

## Research behind the decisions

- TED-Ed's bridge riddle gives the audience a concrete task with a demonstrable solution. A designer of the MIT Mystery Hunt describes revising puzzles after independent testsolves. For 7AI, the practical inference is to test a fair-play episode with the answer concealed and ask the viewer to identify their evidence. Apply that test only when the episode actually promises solvability. [TED-Ed's bridge riddle](https://www.ted.com/talks/alex_gendler_can_you_solve_the_bridge_riddle) · [MIT Mystery Hunt designer's account](https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/two-hundred-puzzles-2/)
- Practical Engineering starts its water-tower explanation with a visible part of a familiar system, then uses operating conditions to explain its function. Mayer and Anderson studied how synchronizing explanatory pictures and narration affected problem solving. For 7AI, inspect whether the part doing the work is visible when the causal sentence is spoken. A viewer's ability to predict a changed outcome is a better editorial test of understanding than recalling the component names. [Practical Engineering](https://practical.engineering/blog/2019/3/9/how-water-towers-work) · [Mayer and Anderson, original experiments](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Explicit%20Instruction/Instructive%20Animation.pdf)
- YouTube describes the opening retention measure, declines, dips and replay spikes. A spike can indicate interest or confusion. After release, compare videos of similar length and viewing context, mark the challenge or mechanism, and inspect losses around the thinking interval and the payoff. For Shorts, use the platform's initial-view and duration measures where available. Neither the sources nor this design establishes an expected lift or a universal reveal timestamp. [YouTube retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en) · [YouTube content analytics by format](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)

## Architecture and release decision

Implement the two records on the consumer-integrated base. Use the existing direction resolver, typed profile, immutable profile saves, shared prompt builder, note, evidence packet and approval gates. The domain part has a special responsibility because it reaches the picture planners. For Puzzle it must tell them to stage the evidence and its return. For Industrial it must tell them to show the operative relationship and resulting state. A script reviewer gets the underlying fairness and causality rules through the shared story, pacing and ending parts.

Keep two channels on the same preset recognizably different. The writer uses the channel's script guide and brief alongside the preset. The still generator takes the planner's scene and applies the channel's positive and negative visual prompts. In the app, the one small presentation change is to fold the whole Creative direction section like Script and Visuals, showing the selected direction in its header and preserving drafts when reopened.

The hardest case is an essential visual detail that the viewer must discover before anyone names it. A planner cannot recover an unspoken clue from a channel label. For a Short, the entire approved narration is available, so the **payoff** can describe the detail that the planner must show earlier. Long planning batches see the current chapter and the chapter titles. Stage a visible premise in the setup chapter, or place setup and verification in the same chapter. Viewers can see a feature or hear its neutral description before they understand its significance. Use the channel note for recurring visual treatment and check the final cut at Gate 2, including opening replacement and cropping. If an exact detail is absent or changes, revise before approval. Reusable source stills or a structured visual brief would be a sensible next investment if matched render evaluations repeatedly fail for this reason.

Use additive keys and preserve the four v1 texts. The duplicated key enum in run creation options needs the shared key tuple. The app and server need a compatibility check before rollout because a downgraded server cannot reliably load a newly saved key.
