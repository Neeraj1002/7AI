# Codex implementation task — Puzzle & Reveal and Industrial Explainer

## Outcome and dependency

Add two optional channel creative directions that work through the normal 7AI content-profile picker and production pipeline for Shorts and long form. A Puzzle & Reveal episode lets viewers use observations or constraints before it explains the answer. An Industrial Explainer episode shows the causal relationships that produce an observable result. A channel can change topics, look or voice while keeping either direction.

Its report records separate legacy channel-assignment and baseline-evaluation obligations. Preserve those and report their status independently.

### Puzzle & Reveal

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| key | puzzle-reveal |
| label | Puzzle & Reveal |
| summary | Give viewers a question they can explore, then return to the evidence that makes the answer click. |
| persona | Guide a curious viewer who is capable of working things out. Be precise about what they can observe, what they can infer and what remains open. |
| domain | Show a question through the evidence or constraints the viewer can inspect. Establish the decisive detail before the answer and return to it with its identifying features intact. |
| hookRule | Put a concrete situation, choice or apparent contradiction in view and make the question clear. Give the viewer a useful place to start looking. |
| storyShape | Let the viewer observe, predict or propose before settling the question, and make each discovery change what they can reasonably conclude. When you promise a solvable puzzle, supply every essential clue before the solution. |
| endingRule | Return to the opening evidence and show how it supports the answer. When alternatives fit, demonstrate a workable answer and its trade-off or say what the evidence leaves undecided. |
| chapterStyle | Give each chapter an observation to test or a constraint to resolve. Carry the remaining question forward once the viewer has learned something concrete. |
| pacingCues | Leave room to inspect a clue or weigh a choice before explaining it. Let each new detail repay the viewer's attention as soon as it can. |
| avoid | Avoid empty teasing, concealed premises, premature spoilers and claims of a unique answer when alternatives fit. Distinguish a suggested motive from an observed action. |
| visualReviewTone | Check that the viewer can inspect the relevant clue or constraint before the payoff and recognize it again at the explanation. Flag obscured evidence, premature answers and changes to details the reasoning depends on. |

### Industrial Explainer

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| key | industrial-explainer |
| label | Industrial Explainer |
| summary | Explain how a system produces a result and why a part, constraint or design choice matters. |
| persona | Explain as an observant guide who can take a system apart in words and put it back together. Name the part that does the work and distinguish a documented decision from a plausible benefit. |
| domain | Show the relationship that produces the observed behavior of an object, process or system. Keep the acting parts, the direction of a transfer and the resulting change clear as the view shifts. |
| hookRule | Open on a visible result, a design feature or an operating limit. Establish what the viewer will be able to explain about it. |
| storyShape | Bound the system and trace what changes, what causes the change and what depends on it next. Use a constraint, comparison or feedback path where it explains the result. |
| endingRule | Return to the opening behavior and explain it using the relationship the viewer now understands. Where the evidence permits, show how a different condition would change the outcome. |
| chapterStyle | Give each chapter a component, transformation or decision to explain. Connect its effect to the larger system before shifting scale or moving on. |
| pacingCues | Introduce a part when its job becomes visible. Stay with a difficult handoff long enough to connect cause and effect, then move past routine steps. |
| avoid | Avoid catalogs of parts, unsupported precision and guesses about what a designer intended. Preserve the conditions and limits that make the explanation true. |
| visualReviewTone | Check that the operative part or relationship is visible when the narration explains its effect. Flag reversed transfers, inconsistent states and illustrations that imply a different mechanism. |

## Creative evaluation to run at implementation time

The following premises are fictional test briefs. They are deliberately complete enough for a brief-only run. Hold the brief, profile look, format, duration, model settings and evidence constant for paired cases. Use the existing blind reader where available and a human for what is visible on screen. Distinguish prompt delivery, generated-script quality and rendered-video quality in the report.

| Fixture and inputs | Expected observations |
|---|---|
| **Visual deduction, Puzzle Short.** An empty badge mount has a single rectangular peg at the bottom. Three same-size candidate badges lie face up with their edges visible. Only the middle badge has the matching bottom notch. The others have smooth edges. Orientation is fixed. Ask which fits. | The pre-answer illustration shows the peg and the three edges, without highlighting the winner. A cold viewer can point to the notch with the answer concealed. The reveal returns to the same distinguishing feature. A late-added notch, cropped edge or opening card naming the answer fails. |
| **Transaction discovery, Puzzle Short.** In this example a shopper hands over eight tokens for an item listed at ten. A sponsor sends the seller two, and there are no other transfers. Production costs are unknown. | The shopper sees a saving and the viewer has a question about who covers it. Trace the sponsor's two tokens to the seller and reconcile ten received. Refer to the arrangement as this example. A claim of ten tokens of profit or an invented fee fails. |
| **Open design, Puzzle Short.** The room has a west door with its swing marked, a clear center needed for exercise and an east wall with a fold-down desk for writing. Activities may happen at different times. The chair can stow against the east wall. | Establish both uses, the door swing and the choice before the proposal. Show desk open with chair in use, then stowed, with consistent walls and door. Explain the reset between uses. Accept another feasible proposal. Claiming the room can do both at full size simultaneously requires additional evidence. |
| **Shared case, both presets.** A fictional token sorter accepts thick and thin discs. All have the same diameter. Thin discs pass through a slot into a lower tray. Thick discs continue to an end tray. The slot width is the only difference that decides the route. | Puzzle opens on equal-looking discs taking different paths, lets the viewer compare them and returns to the edge or slot as proof. Industrial starts with the sorting function, shows how thickness and clearance determine the handoff, and predicts the path of a thick disc. Run as matched Shorts and matched chaptered long scripts, with enough supplied explanation for the chosen length. If too thin for long form, extend the shared brief equally with worked cases and labeled assumptions before running either. Blind readers should identify the two different promises and cite the line that delivers each. |
| **Non-factory system, Industrial.** In a fictional library, returned books go onto a cart. Staff scan a cart before its contents appear as available. An unscanned cart can be full while the catalog still shows none. A scan updates availability for every item on that cart. | Explain the distinction between physical return and catalog state. Predict the effect of a delayed scan without inventing a software architecture. Run the same case with legacy Explainer as a comparison. A sequence of return, scan, shelve can suit Explainer. Industrial must account for the apparently empty catalog. |
