On the afternoon of 4 December 1872, the crew of the Dei Gratia see a ship heading unsteadily toward them, and nobody answers their signals. The helmsman reports her about six miles off. Her sails sit oddly on the grey swell... and Captain David Morehouse begins to suspect that something is wrong.

Who sailed on the Mary Celeste, and where were they going? She was built at Spencer's Island, Nova Scotia, and launched on 18 May 1861 as the Amazon. American owners bought her and renamed her Mary Celeste in 1868. A ten-thousand-dollar refit in early 1872 enlarged her. Captain Benjamin Briggs, born in 1835 in Wareham, Massachusetts, held four of twelve shares. He sailed with his wife Sarah and their two-year-old daughter Sophia. Their son Arthur stayed at home with his grandmother. Seven crew joined them: first mate Albert Richardson, second mate Andrew Gilling, steward Edward Head, and four German seamen, the brothers Volkert and Boz Lorenzen, Arian Martens and Gottlieb Goudschaal. Ten people were aboard. The cargo was 1,701 barrels of alcohol, described as industrial alcohol, bound from New York for Genoa. On 3 November, Briggs wrote to his mother, in a letter quoted by the historian Charles Fay, that "our vessel is in beautiful trim". She left her pier on 5 November, waited off Staten Island for better weather, and entered the Atlantic on 7 November. The Dei Gratia, a brigantine from Nova Scotia loading petroleum at Hoboken for Genoa via Gibraltar, left New York on 15 November, eight days after her.

What did the boarding party find? Morehouse saw no one on deck, and his signals got no reply. So he sent first mate Oliver Deveau and second mate John Wright across by boat.
