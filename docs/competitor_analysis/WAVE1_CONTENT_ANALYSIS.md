# Wave 1 Competitor Content Analysis (v1)

**Date:** 2026-09-25 · **Status:** Draft v1.1 (visual + hook pass done) · **Feeds:** Channel Bibles for Wave 1 (History, Money & Business, Psychology)
**Related:** `CHANNEL_STRATEGY_AND_COMPETITORS.md`

## 0. Method & limits

**How competitors were chosen:**
- Ran 7–10 YouTube Shorts-filtered searches per niche, in two passes: India-region default, then US-region.
- Screened about 90 candidate channels by the **median views of their latest ~48 Shorts**. That median measures a *repeatable format*, not a one-off viral hit.
- Picked 5 per niche. Each set has a benchmark, mid-size channels 7AI could realistically match, and one cautionary example.

**Data pulled per channel:**
- Subscribers, and titles + views of the latest ~48 Shorts.
- For the top 6 recent Shorts and the latest 12: length, publish date, views, likes.
- Upload cadence, derived from the latest 12.

**Title-pattern test:** 644 titles total. Each video's views are measured relative to its own channel's median, then compared across title features.

**Visual and hook pass (v1.1):**
- Reviewed thumbnail sheets for the top 4 Shorts of all 15 channels.
- Took on-player screenshots of 2 top Shorts.
- Read full transcripts of 8 top Shorts through YouTube's transcript panel. Some Shorts have no transcript.

**Caveat:** view counts are a snapshot from 2026-09-25, and search results are personalized. Treat everything here as directional.

## 1. Headline findings

### 1.1 🚨 The generic "psychology facts" format is dead. This changes the Psychology channel.
- Screened 10+ faceless "psychology facts" channels. Their median views were **150–5,300 per Short**:

  | Channel | Subscribers | Median views |
  |---|---|---|
  | Psychology-Facts987 | 69K | ~1K |
  | rshmindtree | 27K | ~1K |
  | PsycheCortex | — | 158 |

- Even the big psychology brands do poorly on Shorts:

  | Channel | Subscribers | Median views |
  |---|---|---|
  | Psych2Go | 13.2M | 52K |
  | Robert Greene | 2.3M | 81K |
  | Big Think | 9M | 42K |

- **What does work nearby:** psychology told as a *curiosity story*:
  - Sean Andrew: median **5.5M**. Examples: "What Human Brains Actually Feel Like" (25.8M), "The Opposite of Déjà Vu is Incredibly Strange" (11M), "Why Humans Create False Memories" (2.2M).
  - Immersive "could you survive / imagine" POV formats: thefossilfacts, median 581K.
- **Implication:** "Psychology & Philosophy" becomes **"Human Mind & Body Curiosities"**. Psychology is the subject; curiosity-story is the format. This is the riskiest Wave 1 channel. See §5.

### 1.2 History has the strongest proof that faceless works
realhistorydude has only **107K subscribers but a 1.9M median**. Examples: "Pablo Escobar Was HORRIFYING!" (20.2M), "Henry The 8th Was DISGUSTING!" (13.0M). The format is animated, about 60s, with one repeatable title formula. This is the closest direct model for 7AI.

### 1.3 Money: underdog brand stories win, but the proof is in Hindi
- The consistent winner is **underdog vs. giant brand stories**:
  - TherichTalkk: "After Betrayed He Built Haldiram's Biggest Competitor" (19.7M)
  - Amann.Sharmaa: "How Balaji Wafers Destroyed Lay's" (4.2M), "How a 10th Fail Boy Built a ₹400 Crore Ice Cream Empire" (7.4M)
- **The transcripts show both faceless winners are narrated in Hindi**, even though their titles are in English. TherichTalkk and Amann.Sharmaa serve India, at low RPM.
- The only English money channel with proof at scale is marktilbury, and it's host-led. So an English, faceless money-story channel is **not yet proven**. We would be the first test.
- **For our English/global channel:** apply the same story structure to global brands. marktilbury's cost-breakdown angle works globally: "Cost vs Price: Nutella Jar" (13.8M), "How Much Gordon Ramsay Makes" (17M).
- **Monetization signal:** TherichTalkk's Shorts carry YouTube Shopping product tags ("Earns commission"), so brand stories double as affiliate placements. The same Shorts show YouTube's **"AI: Content was made with AI"** label and are **auto-dubbed**, and still reach 19.7M views. That's live evidence that labelled AI content isn't suppressed.

### 1.4 Title tricks don't move views. Topic does.
Views relative to channel median, with vs. without each feature:

| Title feature | Psych | Money | History |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emoji | 1.0 vs 0.98 | 1.0 vs 0.96 | (too few) |
| ALL-CAPS word | 1.0 vs 1.0 | 0.88 vs 1.0 | 1.21 vs 0.91 |
| Starts with Why/How/What | 1.0 vs 0.96 | 0.97 vs 1.0 | 1.40 vs 0.92 |
| Contains a number | 0.9 vs 1.0 | 1.1 vs 0.88 | 0.7 vs 1.0 |

- Within a channel, cosmetic title choices barely matter. The one weak exception is "Why/How" titles for History.
- **Outliers come from topic choice:** a famous subject plus a hidden or dark angle (Escobar, Henry VIII, Jack the Ripper, Haldiram vs. Lay's, Nokia, "real dinosaur sounds").
- **Implication for 7AI:** put LLM budget into **Content Prediction (topic selection)**, not title A/B testing.

### 1.5 Winners post less often than you'd think
- Most winners post **0.7–2 Shorts per week**. HistorybyMae (8.4/week, 17–21s videos) is the exception.
- **Implication:** 7AI doesn't need volume, which also keeps us well clear of the "mass-produced" pattern YouTube enforces against. Target 3–4 per week per channel.

### 1.6 Two length modes work
| Mode | Length | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Single-hit | 14–23s | SwishMic, HistorybyMae, newgenytshorts |
| Full story | 55–90s | realhistorydude, HeyHistorically, extrahistory, TherichTalkk, Amann.Sharmaa |

- Median length of top performers: History 60s, Money 41s, Psych 42s.
- Nobody winning uses the new 3-minute limit.
- **Implication:** in the first 30 Shorts per channel, test a 20s format against a 60s format.

### 1.7 Named series build recognition
Recurring series titles are common among winners:
- extrahistory: "… | Worst Dads in History", "… | Recently Deleted History"
- SwishMic: "What's REALLY Inside Your ___?"
- realhistorydude: "___ Was TERRIFYING!"
- newgenytshorts: "This ___ Business Idea is Printing"

**The line 7AI must hold:** these channels vary the *research and visuals* in every episode while the frame stays fixed. The channels YouTube removed kept both the frame and the content near-identical.

### 1.8 AI-first history peaked and stopped; human-credited channels thrive
- AIHistorianOfficial: 335K subscribers, 3M median. It hasn't uploaded since **August 2023**.
- HeyHistorically credits people in every description ("Narration: Youri / Animation: William and Naomi") and averages 5.2M.
- **Implication:** publish human editorial credits in descriptions. It's cheap evidence of "human creative input."

### 1.9 What the winners look and sound like (visual + hook pass)

**Visual styles, and whether 7AI can reproduce them:**

| Style | Who | 7AI-reproducible? |
|---|---|---|
| Caricature of a famous figure in a semi-realistic AI scene, a recurring narrator avatar, one-word pop captions mid-screen, and a sentence caption on top | realhistorydude | ✅ **Best fit.** Image generation + compositing + word-level captions |
| Cute 2D cartoon characters, flat colors | HeyHistorically, extrahistory, Psych2Go | ⚠️ Needs a consistent character model. Animation is the costly part. |
| Bold 2-line headline card ("FROM CHEATED TO 17,000 CRORE") over B-roll or a realistic image of the founder | TherichTalkk | ✅ Easy. ⚠️ Realistic images of real people run into likeness policy, so use illustrations. |
| Cinematic CGI scenes + label captions, driven by sound and little narration | thefossilfacts | ✅ AI video generation + sound design |
| 3D anatomy overlay on a human + label chips ("PEAK PAIN 6–18 HOURS"), no narration, 14s | SwishMic | ✅ Short, cheap, loopable |
| Photoreal AI images + big single-word captions | AIHistorianOfficial | ❌ The *dormant* 2023 AI look. Avoid. |
| Host on camera | Sean Andrew, marktilbury, HistorybyMae, Amann.Sharmaa (partly) | ❌ Not faceless |
| Clips of others' podcasts, films, or viral footage | money.talks0, CoreGenZ, newgenytshorts | ❌ Reused-content / copyright risk |

**Spoken hooks, from transcripts:**

| Short | Views | Opening line | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pablo Escobar Was HORRIFYING! | 20.2M | "Pablo Escobar was a lot scarier than you previously thought. First off…" | Claim → escalating list → **"But it gets much worse"** → climax → death/ending. About 170 words in 61s. |
| Henry The 8th Was DISGUSTING! | 13.0M | "King Henry VIII was probably the most disgusting person in history. First off…" | Same template. Ends on the most shocking detail ("his body exploded"). 235 words. |
| 60s before NUKE (Japan) | 25.3M | "…before the nuke detonates. An old couple in Hiroshima is finishing breakfast." | **Countdown framing** ("50 seconds… 40 seconds…"). Intimate characters, then catastrophe. 194 words. |
| What Human Brains Actually Feel Like | 25.8M | "Do you know what your brain feels like? Well, it's way different than most people think." | Question → misconception → correction → vivid tactile payoff ("Jell-O and warm butter"). 83 words in 31s. |
| After Betrayed He Built Haldiram's Biggest Competitor | 19.7M | (Hindi) "When Haldiram's grandson was betrayed, how did he build its biggest competitor…" | Title restated as a question → origin → betrayal → comeback → today's revenue figure. 178 words. |
| How a 10th Fail Boy Built a ₹400 Crore Ice Cream Empire | 7.4M | (Hindi) "How a boy who washed dirty plates built a ₹400 crore ice-cream company." | Humble origin → observation → idea → today's scale. 196 words. |
| How Much Gordon Ramsay Makes | 17.0M | "How much do Gordon Ramsay's restaurants make?" | Question → estimate → "Fact check it" → reveal. 55 words. |
| Dinosaurs Didn't Roar… | 8.3M | Almost no speech: screams and sound effects | Sound is the hook. |

**Hook rules that follow from these:**
1. **The first sentence names the famous subject *and* makes a claim** ("X was a lot scarier than you thought", "X is way different than most people think"). No "Hey guys", no context-setting.
2. **Correct a misconception** ("than most people think", "than you previously thought"). This shows up in the top psych and history hits.
3. **Re-hook in the middle** with an escalation line ("But it gets much worse", "But it gets even weirder").
4. **End on the single most shocking or concrete detail**, or on a number (revenue, death toll). No call to action.
5. **Pace:** about 160–180 words per minute. A 60s Short is about 170 words; a 30s Short is about 85.

**Patterns to adopt:**
- **Title formulas:**
  - "[Famous figure] Was [TERRIFYING/DISGUSTING/EVIL]!"
  - "How [group] survived [danger]"
  - "Why [surprising historical behavior]?"
- **Topics:** famous villains and rulers, bizarre survival methods, "what if", tie-ins to news or anniversaries. The Trend agent can feed the last one.
- **Visual anchors:** a caricature of the famous figure in a realistic scene (realhistorydude), or simple character animation (HeyHistorically, extrahistory). Public-domain art can be used as cutaways.
- **Avoid:** gore, since these topics sit near advertiser limits. Also the saturated generic WWII and "weird history lists."

## 5. Implications

### 5.1 Revised Wave 1 ranking (evidence changed it)
| Rank | Channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dark/Untold History** | Strongest faceless proof (realhistorydude), repeatable formulas, public-domain visuals |
| 2 | **Money & Business Stories** | Proven story structures and the highest ad rates, plus Shopping affiliate income. ⚠️ The faceless proof is in Hindi; English faceless is untested. |
| 3 | **Human Mind & Body Curiosities** (reframed Psychology) | The formats are proven (thefossilfacts, SwishMic are faceless and AI-reproducible), but the biggest psych hits are host-led. Gate it hard after 30 Shorts. |

> **Open decision:** Money could launch as **Hindi narration with English titles**, copying the proven format, with YouTube auto-dub for reach. That trades RPM for certainty. My recommendation: stay English, but treat the first 30 Shorts as a real experiment with a clear kill/pivot rule.

### 5.2 Inputs for the Channel Bibles
| Channel Bible field | History | Money | Mind & Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core format | Famous figure/event + dark or surprising angle | Underdog-vs-giant / origin / cost breakdown | "Why humans…" / "What it feels like…" |
| Length test | 20s vs 60s | 30s vs 55s | 15s vs 40s |
| Cadence | 3–4/wk | 3–4/wk | 3–4/wk |
| Visual anchor | Caricature of the figure in a semi-real scene, word-pop captions | Headline card + illustrated founder/brand + B-roll | Cinematic CGI / 3D anatomy + label chips |
| Hook template | "[Figure] was [worse] than you thought. First off…" → "But it gets worse" | Title-as-question → humble origin → twist → today's number | Question → misconception → correction → tactile payoff |
| Words per Short | ~170 (60s) | ~150–190 (50–60s) | ~85 (30s) or none (14s label-only) |
| Hard no's | Gore, generic WWII lists | Reused footage, celebrity dark secrets | Facts lists, dark psychology, diagnosis |
| Human credit line | Yes | Yes | Yes |

### 5.3 Pipeline implications
- **Content Prediction first:** since topic drives the outliers, the topic-selection agent matters more than title optimization. It can come out of MVP deferral, starting simple: a list of famous subjects × angle templates.
- **Script Review rubric:** check for a *named, famous subject* in the first 3 seconds, plus a specific surprising claim, plus a fact source.
- **Competitor watchlist:** these 15 handles become the seed list for Video Intelligence (`competitor` mode). Re-pull medians weekly.
